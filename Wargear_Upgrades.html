Wargear Upgrades

Sanctified · Artificer · Master-Crafted Patterns
◇ How Wargear Upgrades Work

Beyond the standard Requisitorium catalogue sit the Sanctioned Upgrade Patterns — permanent enhancements bonded to a single weapon or piece of armour through ritual maintenance, artificer's work, and consecrated components. Any character can requisition an upgrade for a weapon or armour piece they already own, applied during a resupply at an Armoury, Forge, or equivalent facility. This is a permanent choice: the upgrade is bonded to that specific physical item. If the item is lost or destroyed, the Requisition Points invested in it are lost with it.

There are three tiers, purchased in sequence — you cannot skip a tier. Each tier grants a flat numeric bonus (+1 / +2 / +3 to attack and damage rolls for weapons, or +1 / +2 / +3 AC for armour) exactly like upgrading a magic item's plus in 5e. Each tier also unlocks one Augmetic Trait slot — a named special property chosen from that tier's list (or any lower tier's list). Traits are cumulative: a Master-Crafted (Tier 3) item retains every trait chosen at Tier 1 and Tier 2, plus its own Tier 3 pick, for three traits total.

Swapping traits: Augmetic Traits can only be swapped between missions, during the downtime that follows a completed mission. Swapping out one previously chosen trait for a different one your item qualifies for costs 50 RP × the tier of the trait being installed — 50 RP for a Tier I trait, 100 RP for Tier II, 150 RP for Tier III. The old trait's blessing is stripped and the new one consecrated in its place. Outside of a Techmarine or Tech-Priest's field work (see below), mid-mission swaps are not possible, even at a resupply.

Restrictions: upgrades cannot be applied to Relic-category items — those are already singular artefacts with their own fixed properties (a GM may allow narrative exceptions for a major storyline). An item can only carry one tier track; you don't stack Tier I and Tier II as separate purchases; buying Tier II simply raises the same item to +2 and grants its second trait slot.

Techmarines and Tech-Priests apply a 25% discount (round down to the nearest 5 RP) to every upgrade cost above, including trait swap costs, reflecting their own tools and standing with the Mechanicus priesthood. They are also the only characters who may install a Tier I upgrade or perform a trait swap in the field during a short rest, rather than waiting for downtime between missions, provided they have Artificer's Tools on hand. Tier II and Tier III installations still require proper Armoury/Forge access regardless of class. Any character proficient with Artificer's Tools or equivalent smith's tools receives a smaller 10% discount on installation and swap costs.

This system runs alongside, not in place of, the Techmarine's Mechanicus Enhancement class feature — that remains a temporary per-resupply buff layered on top of whatever permanent upgrade tier the item already carries. None of the traits below duplicate or outclass an existing class's signature feature — where an idea overlapped (Terminator's Ceramite Wall, Close Combat Operative's Ceramite Bulwark, Gravis Operative's Iron Halo/Unyielding Platform, or the Hexagrammic Ward relic), it was reworked into a distinct niche instead.

Weapon-Family Traits: a number of Augmetic Traits are restricted to specific weapon patterns — they graft an existing named weapon property (drawn straight from the Requisitorium's own modifier list) onto a compatible weapon that doesn't already have it. A Las Fusil can requisition an Overcharge Coil because Las weapons already run this exact risk in their lore; a Bolt weapon cannot, because there's no in-fiction mechanism for a solid mass-reactive round to "overcharge." These traits still occupy a normal trait slot at the tier listed and cost nothing beyond reaching that tier — they're restricted by eligibility, not by extra price. A weapon can never take a Family Trait for a property it already has baked into its base mods list. GM has final say on borderline cases (a homebrew Las-pattern rifle added later, for instance, should qualify for the same Las-family traits).

Armor Pattern Traits: likewise, each of the four Armor Patterns (Phobos, Tacticus, Gravis, and Terminator) has two Augmetic Traits available only to that pattern, reflecting its distinct construction and role on the battlefield. Like Weapon-Family Traits, these occupy a normal trait slot at the tier listed with no extra RP cost, and require both the matching armor pattern and proficiency to wear it.

On your character sheet: every upgrade below can be installed directly from the sheet — click the ✦ button on a weapon or armour row in the Wargear panel to open its Upgrade Console, set the tier, and consecrate traits into its slots. Attack, damage, AC, ammo capacity, and crit range adjust automatically.
