GENESTEALER CULT BESTIARY

Warhammer 40,000 × Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition — Homebrew

This bestiary catalogues the hidden hierarchy of the Genestealer Cult — the xenos infection that precedes the Hive Fleet, growing in the dark for generations before the Rising is called. Unlike the overt bioforms of the Tyranid swarm, the Genestealer Cult is a weapon of shadow and patience: hybrids bred across generations, indoctrinated minds, and ancient alien hunters lurking within the very civilizations they are destined to consume. Entries are arranged from lowest to highest Challenge Rating, from the most common Neophyte foot soldier to the ancient horror of the Patriarch itself. Pure Tyranid bioforms are catalogued in the Tyranid Bestiary.

These are fan-made rules for personal use only.

Designer's Notes: Key Genestealer Cult Mechanics

Brood Telepathy. All Genestealer Cult creatures share a 60-foot psychic bond through tendrils of the Hive Mind. This silent communication cannot be detected or blocked by mundane means — cult forces coordinate perfectly in silence, cannot be deceived by false auditory signals, and relay tactical information instantaneously. The Nexos extends this further via Pheromone Intelligence: it knows the precise position of every friendly cult creature within 120 feet, even through solid walls, making flanks and ambushes almost impossible to conceal from an experienced cult force.

Gene-seed Implant. Purestrain Genestealers can implant a gene-seed instead of dealing normal damage on a Rending Claws hit (once per day). The target must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or become infected. The infection lies dormant for 1d4 weeks before manifesting as alien physical traits — and can be cured by remove curse or equivalent magic before full manifestation. This mechanic is best used sparingly for dramatic effect; an infected player character carries a countdown that fundamentally changes the atmosphere of subsequent sessions.

The Patriarch's Dominion. The Patriarch is less a combat creature than a reality-warping authority — and its mechanics reflect that. Patriarch's Presence grants every cult creature within 60 feet an extra 1d10 damage on all attacks, turning even basic Neophytes into serious threats. Progenitor's Will allows it to survive a killing blow once per encounter by rising to 60 hit points — unless that blow was a critical hit. Patriarch's Kiss permanently charms a grappled target on a failed DC 19 Constitution save (treated as geas, removed by 5th-level remove curse), and is the most consequential single ability in the entire bestiary.

Cult Command Hierarchy. The Genestealer Cult is defined by its command structure, and encounters scale dramatically based on which leaders are present. The Nexos grants one ally an additional action each round (initiative count 20) and designates Priority Targets that give all cult creatures advantage on attacks. The Magus dominates minds with a DC 17 Wisdom save and projects psychic phantoms for cover. Players who identify and eliminate these command figures early — Nexos, Magus, Primus — will find the cult's cohesion collapse; those who ignore them will face a force that fights well above its numbers.

Neophyte Hybrid

Neophyte Hybrids are among the most human-looking members of the Genestealer Cult — indistinguishable from ordinary citizens until the dormant alien genome begins to assert itself. They hold legitimate jobs in mines, manufactorums, and docking crews, quietly building the cult's power base for years or decades before the Rising is called. Armed with autoguns and autopistols scavenged or purchased through the cult's hidden networks, they form the rank-and-file infantry of the uprising.

What makes the Neophyte truly dangerous is not individual fighting ability but the sheer scale of infiltration. An Imperial city hosting a mature Genestealer Cult may have thousands of Neophytes occupying positions throughout every level of society — PDF troopers, administratum clerks, Arbites officers. When the Patriarch issues the call through the brood's psychic bond, they all rise simultaneously. Planetary defenses crumble in hours, not because Neophytes are exceptional warriors, but because the defenders never realized the war was already inside the walls.

Acolyte Hybrid

Acolyte Hybrids stand several generations closer to the Genestealer bloodline than their Neophyte kin, and it shows. Their alien heritage is unmistakable — too-wide mouths, additional limbs, eyes that catch light in the dark — and they no longer pass in polite society. They live in the hidden depths of the cult, training in the shadows of sub-levels and underhive warrens, waiting for the uprising they know is inevitable.

Despite their monstrous appearance, Acolyte Hybrids are fanatically devoted — not merely conditioned, but genuinely loving toward the Patriarch whose gene-seed flows in their veins. They form the assault core of Genestealer Cult forces, closing with enemies in tight melee where their alien physiology becomes lethal advantage. Pack tactics come naturally to creatures linked by psychic bond, and Acolyte squads fight with uncanny coordination, each reacting to threats as if sharing a single mind.

Hybrid Metamorph

Hybrid Metamorphs represent the pinnacle of controlled Genestealer mutation — hybrids whose alien genes have expressed in strange and formidable ways. Extra limbs have grown and hardened into weapon-like appendages; the body has twisted into configurations that defy human expectation while being supremely suited for violence. Unlike the uncontrolled Aberrant, the Metamorph's mutations are purposeful, guided by the Biophagus's expertise in mutagenic compounds.

Metamorphs are used as shock assault units — dropped into the midst of enemy formations to create chaos. Their additional limbs allow them to attack from unexpected angles, striking with a bludgeoning appendage from one side while raking with blades from another. The prehensile tendrils that writhe from their shoulders can disarm opponents, tangle weapons, or pin a struggling enemy while the primary limbs do the actual killing. Veteran soldiers who survive Metamorph charges typically advise one thing: "Don't watch the claws — it's the thing you don't see coming that kills you."

Aberrant

Aberrants are what happens when the Genestealer gene-seed overwhelms the human host's biology without the benefit of the Biophagus's stabilizing compounds. The resulting creature is enormous, massively over-muscled, and barely capable of higher thought — a killing machine of prodigious size and fury that the cult keeps kenneled until battle is joined. They are the blunt instrument: walls of muscle hurled at fortified positions and armored vehicles with equal enthusiasm.

Despite their apparent lack of intellect, Aberrants are bound to the Patriarch by the same psychic brood-bond that links all cult members. Through this connection they receive crude impressions of will — fight, protect, destroy — that guide them where they are most needed. The cult's leadership has learned to use Aberrants like siege weapons: point them at an obstacle and release them. A pair of Aberrants can topple a Leman Russ tank through sheer applied force, tearing sponson guns free and hammering the hull until the crew inside are killed by concussive trauma alone.

Purestrain Genestealer

The Purestrain Genestealer is the original horror — the creature from which all hybrid bloodlines ultimately descend, carrying no human genetics whatsoever. These ancient predators are deployed by Hive Fleets as advance infiltrators, capable of surviving in vacuum, extreme cold, and hostile atmospheres for extended periods. Their armored carapace deflects small-arms fire while their rending claws can tear through combat plate like paper, and a single successful gene-seed implant can begin a chain reaction capable of bringing down a planetary civilization within a few generations.

A Purestrain is not a beast operating on simple instinct — it is a predator of alien intelligence, capable of concealing itself in a starship's darkened vents for months, choosing precisely when to hunt and when to remain perfectly still as armed search teams pass within arm's reach. The creature that infects a mine overseer this season may, through that overseer's infected children and grandchildren, command a planet-wide uprising a century hence. Every Genestealer Patriarch began as one of these: patient, purposeful, and utterly without mercy.

Biophagus

The Biophagus is the Genestealer Cult's alchemist-savant — a hybrid whose alien intellect has been turned toward the science of mutation. Through years of careful experimentation with synthetic mutagenic compounds derived from the Patriarch's gene-fluid, the Biophagus refines and stabilizes the hybrids it treats, coaxing out useful mutations rather than the uncontrolled aberrations that arise in their absence. Its hidden laboratory is filled with vials of amber fluid, experimental specimens, and the occasional failed subject that never quite reached completion.

In battle the Biophagus fulfills a support role, administering combat stimulants to nearby hybrids while deploying acidic toxins against enemies. But it is most valued for what it does between battles: the Biophagus determines which hybrids receive mutagenic treatment and in what dose, effectively selecting who becomes an Acolyte, who becomes a Metamorph, and who is sacrificed in experimental procedures. This power makes the Biophagus second in influence only to the Magus and Primus in most cults, despite its limited combat capability. Few cult members dare defy someone who decides what they will become.

Nexos

The Nexos is not a warrior — it is a brain. This hyper-evolved hybrid possesses a compound mind capable of processing dozens of incoming data streams simultaneously: pheromone signals from nearby brood-kin, tactical assessments, structural scans of terrain, probabilistic calculations of enemy response times. It serves as the cult's living command-and-control system, transforming the Patriarch's broad psychic imperatives into precise tactical instructions that every hybrid in the field can act upon.

The Nexos carries crystalline data-looms that interface directly with its alien nervous system, allowing it to model battlefield scenarios in real time. A cult with an active Nexos fights with terrifying efficiency — squads arriving at precisely the right moment, ambushes triggered with perfect timing, reinforcements flowing through routes the enemy hasn't thought to cover. Imperial tacticians who have faced cults with Nexos support describe a disturbing sensation: the feeling that every decision they make has already been anticipated, that they are not choosing their responses so much as walking a path that was laid for them months in advance.

Abominant

The Abominant is what the Biophagus achieves when it finds an Aberrant with sufficient genetic potential and applies the full suite of mutagenic enhancement to it. The result is even larger and more powerful than a standard Aberrant, and crucially — slightly more tractable. The Abominant carries a massive power hammer that it swings with bone-crushing enthusiasm, and even heavy vehicles have learned to fear it. A single Abominant can raze a fortified blockhouse before the defenders have time to call for reinforcements.

Creating an Abominant is a significant resource investment, and there are rarely more than one or two per brood. They are treated with something approaching reverence by other cult members — creatures of primal awe to the hybrids who witness what unconstrained Genestealer genetics can become. In battle they march at the front of Aberrant formations, their power hammers clearing lanes through barricades, blast doors, and whatever else stands between the cult and its objective. When an Abominant charges a fortified line, the question is not whether the line will break — only how long it takes.

Kelermorph

The Kelermorph is a legend given flesh — a three-gunned killer of extraordinary speed and precision whose deeds are whispered with reverence throughout the brood. Part assassin, part icon, the Kelermorph exists to remove high-value targets and inspire the cult's rank-and-file through acts of impossible marksmanship. Three liberator autopistols hang at its sides; when the fighting starts, all three fire in a blur almost too fast for the eye to follow, each finding a separate target across the room before the first shell casing hits the floor.

Kelermorphs are not simply fast draws. They have studied their targets — studied routes, habits, preferences — and by the time they fire the first shot, the outcome has already been decided by hours of preparation. To witness a Kelermorph in action is to watch something that appears almost supernatural: ducking through crossfire that should be lethal, firing backward over one shoulder while vaulting a barricade, ventilating three separate targets before any of them registers the threat. The cult's enemies call this luck. The Kelermorph's brood-kin know it to be the product of alien senses and decades of ruthless training.

Locus

The Locus is the Magus's shadow — a personal bodyguard of such speed and reflexes that it makes dedicated close protection look effortless. It is always within five paces of its charge, scanning for threats with senses heightened far beyond human baseline, ready to interpose itself between the Magus and any incoming violence in an eyeblink. It cannot be ordered away, bribed, deceived, or frightened from its post. To reach the Magus, one must first go through the Locus.

The bond between Locus and Magus is more than professional duty — it is psychic imprinting as deep as the brood-bond itself. The Locus knows the Magus's heartbeat, can sense a shift in its biorhythms that signals distress, and will cross a battlefield in seconds in response to a threat the Magus itself hasn't consciously registered. Enemies who eliminate the Magus while the Locus is nearby rarely survive the experience; the creature's response to its principal's death is an uncontrolled explosion of lethal precision that even seasoned warriors describe as less a fight and more a massacre.

Sanctus

The Sanctus is the Genestealer Cult's dedicated assassin — specifically evolved and trained to kill psykers. Its alien biology contains a pheromone profile that disrupts psychic resonance, creating a null-zone that makes the Warp feel distant and unreliable to those who depend on it. Sanctuses seek out Primaris Psykers, Astropaths, Sorcerers, and any other target whose death will cripple an enemy's command structure, operating alone in hostile territory for months without resupply or psychic support.

The Sanctus is cold in a way that most Genestealer Hybrids are not. Where others feel the warmth of the brood-bond and the Patriarch's psychic love, the Sanctus carries its null-aura like a second skin — isolated even within the cult's psychic communion. This isolation is deliberately cultivated; the Sanctus must be able to function alone, deep in enemy territory, without any of the comforts of the hive-mind link. What emerges from this isolation is a creature of perfect patience and absolute lethality. It will wait. It will watch. When it finally fires, it will not miss.

Jackal Alphus

The Jackal Alphus leads the cult's cavalry elements — the swift Jackal riders who serve as scouts, outriders, and rapid-response elements. The Alphus itself rides at the head of these formations, alternating between lightning-fast scouting maneuvers and long-range precision fire that pins down enemy elements while the rest of the cult advances. Where other cult officers command through the brood-bond, the Alphus commands through the scope of a precision rifle, painting targets for the forces flowing behind it.

The Alphus's primary value is target designation — a skill that transforms the cult's disparate elements into a focused killing machine. When the Alphus marks a target through its scope, that target's death is rarely a matter of whether, only of which angle the killing blow arrives from. Commanders who have engaged cults with an Alphus in the field report one consistent experience: the enemy always seems to know exactly where to aim, responding to movements that should have been impossible to observe. It is not magic — it is decades of riding these tunnels and streets and knowing every line of sight before the battle begins.

Primus

The Primus is the cult's warlord — the master of arms who translates the Patriarch's psychic will into battlefield doctrine. Where the Magus shapes minds and the Patriarch projects alien authority, the Primus kills. It is the apex combatant of the Genestealer Cult, a blade-wielder of extraordinary skill who leads the assault personally when the moment calls for it. Even without its tactical gifts, a Primus would be one of the most dangerous melee fighters in any room it enters.

The Primus plans the uprising's military phase with methodical precision, knowing every Imperial garrison strength, every patrol route, every command bunker's security code — because it spent years, in near-human form, building relationships with the soldiers it will one day order its brood to slaughter. This is the Genestealer Cult's deepest cruelty: the moment of betrayal comes from people the victims trusted, fought alongside, shared meals with. When the Primus steps forward, blade drawn, from among the officers it has served beside for years, the shock of recognition is frequently the last emotion those officers ever experience.

Magus

The Magus is the Genestealer Cult's living conduit to the alien — a hybrid whose psychic potential has been nurtured to extraordinary levels, blazing with xenos power that the Imperium classifies somewhere between psyker and entity. It is the Patriarch's mouthpiece, prophet, and priest, translating the ancient Genestealer's alien directives into comprehensible spiritual doctrine for the cult's lower orders. Its hypnotic gaze can break an unshielded mind in seconds, and its command of the brood-link makes it the keystone around which the entire cult's spiritual life is organized.

The Magus serves a dual function as both weapon and priest. In peacetime it leads the cult's religious ceremonies, reinforcing the brood-bond through ritual and maintaining the devotion of hybrids who have never met the Patriarch face to face. In battle it is a psychic engine of terrifying capability — mass-charming enemy forces, dominating key individuals, and feeding real-time intelligence back to the Patriarch through their shared psychic link. Cults that lack a Magus often struggle with internal cohesion; cults led by a powerful one become something approaching perfect instruments of the Hive Fleet's will.

Broodlord

The Broodlord is what a Purestrain Genestealer becomes after centuries of predation and gene-expression — massive, ancient, and terrifying in a way that goes beyond the merely physical. It looms over its brood at nearly the size of a Space Marine in Terminator plate, its carapace scarred by centuries of combat, its rending talons capable of tearing through the thickest armor as if through parchment. It is the alpha of alphas: the exemplar of what all Genestealer biology aspires toward.

Broodlords are found at the head of the most mature and powerful Genestealer infestations, typically acting as the Patriarch's chief enforcer and the primary military threat of any assault. Where the Patriarch directs, the Broodlord leads. Its psychic presence elevates every nearby hybrid: brood-kin fight harder, strike truer, and endure more when the Broodlord's ancient alien will radiates through the pheromone link. Planetary defense forces that train specifically for Genestealer Cult uprisings brief their soldiers with one standing order: if you see the large one — the one that doesn't die to normal fire — fall back immediately and call for orbital support.

Genestealer Patriarch

The Genestealer Patriarch is the source of everything — the first Genestealer to set foot on this world, potentially centuries before the cult's uprising, swollen now by decades of gene-energy from its brood into something barely recognizable as the creature it once was. Enormous and ancient, it crouches at the heart of the cult's most sacred sanctum like a living idol, radiating alien psychic presence that holds thousands of devotees in perfect thrall. To look upon it is to feel something deep in the hindbrain that evolution never quite managed to fully suppress: the recognition of an apex predator older and stranger than anything humanity has ever faced.

The Patriarch does not fight as other creatures fight — it fights as a god fights, with absolute certainty that everything in its presence exists to serve its purpose. Its rending claws carry centuries of muscle-memory that makes it a terrifyingly efficient killer even by the standards of the bioforms it has outlived. But it is the psychic presence — the Patriarch's Kiss, the ancient dominating gaze that compels devotion from even the most iron-willed minds — that makes it truly apocalyptic at close range. Scholars who have studied Genestealer Cult uprisings note one consistent observation: the cults with the oldest Patriarchs are always the most dangerous. Centuries of psychic feeding and gene-seed propagation make the ancient ones into something that barely belongs to the mortal world at all.
