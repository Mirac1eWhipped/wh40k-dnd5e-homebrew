TYRANID BESTIARY

Warhammer 40,000 × Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition — Homebrew

This bestiary catalogues the bioforms of the Great Devourer, translated into the D&D 5th Edition ruleset for use at the tabletop. Entries are arranged from lowest to highest Challenge Rating. Each entry includes the creature's lore, tactical role within the Tyranid swarm, and the threat it poses on the battlefield. Genestealer variants are catalogued separately.

These are fan-made rules for personal use only.

Designer's Notes: Key Tyranid Mechanics

Synapse & The Hive Mind. The central Tyranid mechanic. Most low-CR bioforms carry Synapse Dependency — if no Synapse creature (Tyranid Warriors, Primes, Hive Tyrants, etc.) is within 30 feet at the start of their turn, they must succeed on a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw or spend their action attacking the nearest creature regardless of allegiance. Synapse creatures project a 30-foot aura that automatically suppresses those saves and grants nearby Tyranids advantage on saves against the frightened condition. Killing synapse creatures first is a tactically valid and rewarding approach; a swarm severed from its commanders rapidly collapses into chaotic infighting.

Bio-weapons. Tyranid weapons are living organisms, each with its own secondary effect. The Fleshborer fires beetle larvae that burrow into flesh — on a failed Constitution saving throw the target continues taking poison damage each turn until the save is passed. Spinefists can be swept in a wide arc, targeting two adjacent creatures with a single attack roll. Larger bio-weapons (Venom Cannon, Barbed Strangler, Exocrine's Bio-acid Spray) carry individual area and control effects detailed in their respective stat blocks.

Psychic Tyranids. Zoanthropes and Neurothropes project a Psychic Aegis — a warp-field that constitutes their entire AC and grants resistance to nonmagical physical damage. This field collapses if they are incapacitated, and collapses and reforms the following turn after exceptional damage on the Neurothrope variant. Unlike other Tyranids, these creatures use no conventional weapons; their Psychic Shriek is a high-damage area stun. The Neurothrope's Hive Mind Node trait further extends its reach, allowing nearby Synapse-dependent creatures to substitute its Intelligence modifier (+4) on their Synapse Dependency saving throws.

Hazardous Biology. Several Tyranids carry body chemistry that punishes direct engagement. The Pyrovore's Acid Blood sprays caustic fluid on adjacent creatures whenever it takes piercing or slashing damage from a melee attack; its Volatile Metabolism causes it to detonate on death, dealing fire and acid damage in a 15-foot radius. Players who commit melee fighters to these creatures should understand the risk — or learn it the hard way.

Spore Mine

Spore Mines are hollow, gas-filled bio-organisms grown in the body cavities of Biovores and Harpies, then expelled as living ammunition. They drift on thermal currents and chemical instinct toward the nearest warm body, detonating in a burst of caustic bio-acid on contact. They have no will of their own — only hunger encoded into their most basic biology.

First encountered by Imperial forces during the early invasions of Hive Fleet Behemoth, Spore Mines were initially dismissed as a curiosity by commanders unfamiliar with Tyranid warfare. That assessment changed when entire defensive corridors were rendered impassable by cascading chain detonations. A single Biovore can seed a battlefield with dozens of drifting mines across one engagement, and a Harpy in transit drops them like breadcrumbs along its flight path. Even after the mines detonate, the caustic residue lingers — a grim reminder that the ground beneath a Tyranid advance is never truly safe.

Hormagaunt

Hormagaunts are the swarm's foremost shock infantry — lean, fast, and built entirely for melee. They are grown in their millions and flung headlong at enemy lines, relying on Pack Tactics and sheer weight of numbers to overwhelm defenders before they can set formation. Their scything forelimbs can strip armor from bone in seconds.

Within the Synapse field of a Warrior or higher bioform, Hormagaunts fight with almost eerie coordination, their Pack Tactics instincts perfectly exploited by the Hive Mind's direction. Cut off from that field, they become erratic and territorial, as liable to attack one another as the enemy — a behavioral artifact of the raw predatory programming that lies beneath their Synapse conditioning. Soldiers who have survived Hormagaunt charges describe the sound as a continuous chittering roar, like a living wave that simply does not stop. No matter how many fall, the wave does not slow — because the Hive Mind feels no loss for individual gaunts any more than a man notices the death of a single blood cell.

Ripper Swarm

Rippers are the Hive Fleet's final-stage consumers — tiny, tooth-filled horrors that swarm in the thousands over fallen battlefields, stripping every trace of organic matter back to raw biomass for reclamation through the Hive Mind. They are mindless even by Tyranid standards, operating on pure chemically-encoded hunger. A Ripper Swarm is not a threat to be fought; it is a deadline.

What makes the Ripper truly horrifying is not its individual ferocity but its purpose in the grand tapestry of consumption. Each Ripper carries enzymes within its saliva that accelerate organic breakdown — a fallen warrior dissolved to nutrient slurry within minutes, gene-seed, armor, and memory all fed back into the Hive Mind's endless hunger. Civilizations that have repelled a Tyranid assault sometimes find their victory hollow; the Rippers came anyway, and nothing remains of their dead to bury. The swarm does not leave graveyards. It leaves silence.

Termagant

Termagants form the ranged backbone of the Tyranid swarm's first wave. They are small, quick, and prolific — spawned in enormous numbers by Tervigons and deployed ahead of the main assault to lay down suppressive bio-weapon fire. Their Fleshborer weapons fire needle-like beetles that burrow into flesh and release neurotoxins, tearing apart formations before heavier bioforms close to melee range.

Unlike the mindless Ripper, the Termagant possesses a rudimentary tactical awareness granted through the Synapse network. Under a Warrior's oversight it will instinctively fall back when flanked, redirect fire toward priority targets, and press forward when the synapse signal surges with predatory hunger. Stripped of that signal it reverts to feral panic — but in the presence of strong synapse direction, a Termagant brood moves with eerie, almost drilled coordination. Tervigon-mothers can replenish their broods mid-battle, birthing clutches of fresh Termagants onto the field in glistening bursts, ensuring the tide of chitin and bio-acid never truly ebbs.

Gargoyle

Gargoyles are the airborne equivalent of the Termagant — winged, fast, and designed to harass from angles that ground troops cannot easily retaliate against. They screech from above, unleashing Fleshborer fire at enemies cowering behind cover before diving to spray blinding venom. Wherever a Tyranid swarm advances, Gargoyles swarm the sky overhead to deny high ground and eliminate snipers.

Gargoyles are grown with hollow, membrane-stretched wings analogous to the great flying predators of doomed worlds. Their Fleshborer is a symbiotic organism fused to the forearm, indistinguishable from the creature's own body. The venom glands clustered at the back of the throat are the Hive Mind's refinement of an ambush predator's instinct — a pressurized spray of corrosive bio-fluid that can blind a target in an instant, leaving it helpless as the swarm descends. Gargoyle broods are among the first Tyranid organisms sighted during a planetary invasion, darkening skies above cities days before the ground advance reaches the walls.

Biovore

The Biovore is a living mortar — a bloated, immobile creature that births Spore Mines within its body cavity and expels them with powerful muscular contractions at targets hundreds of feet distant. It does not participate in direct combat willingly; it is a fire-support organism, sheltering behind the advance of the swarm while delivering biological ordinance wherever the Hive Mind directs.

The Biovore's symbiotic relationship with its Spore Mines is one of the most sophisticated expressions of Tyranid bio-engineering. The mines are not manufactured — they are gestated, grown inside the Biovore's abdomen as semi-independent organisms that share a rudimentary neural link with their parent. When expelled, they drift on chemical gradients, homing toward the heat and scent signatures of non-Tyranid life. The Biovore can feel through them, sensing when one drifts close to prey and triggering detonation at precisely the right moment. In prolonged sieges, Biovores have been observed launching mines in coordinated patterns — walking barrages that herd panicked defenders directly into the advance of the ground swarm.

Von Ryan's Leaper

Named for the infamous ambush that slaughtered an Imperial command staff on Von Ryan's World, these Tyranid predators are sleek, fast-twitch hunters built entirely for shock attacks. They operate in coordinated packs, using biological instinct and Hive Mind direction to triangulate and pile onto single targets from multiple angles simultaneously. Their powerful rear limbs can launch them across distances that seem physically impossible.

Von Ryan's Leapers are compact hunters whose musculature is dedicated almost entirely to explosive acceleration and rapid directional change. Their exoskeleton is light relative to larger bioforms — the Hive Mind sacrificed durability in favor of speed, creating a creature that wins fights not through brute strength but through the overwhelming trauma of a perfectly coordinated multi-angle assault. When a pack locks onto a target, each Leaper approaches from a different vector, communicating through subsonic vibration and pheromone bursts, arriving simultaneously so that no single blow can be braced against. The name "Von Ryan's Leaper" has since passed into Imperial military vernacular as shorthand for any ambush conducted too perfectly to survive.

Ravener

Raveners are serpentine horrors built for subterranean assault — sinuous, multi-limbed tunnel-hunters that tear through soil and rock to erupt without warning behind defensive lines. They leave functional tunnels in their wake that the broader swarm can exploit, effectively importing Tyranid forces into the heart of an enemy formation. A Ravener does not charge from the front; it appears from below.

The Ravener's body plan is a masterpiece of Tyranid bio-engineering adapted to three-dimensional subterranean warfare. A rigid but flexible exoskeletal helix runs the length of its torso, rotating like a living drill bit as the creature bores forward. Multiple pairs of digging limbs alternate between anchoring and driving, allowing it to maintain speed through soil, rubite, and compacted clay alike. The tunnels it leaves behind carry faint pheromone trails — invisible beacons that other swarm organisms detect and follow. Defenders who destroy a Ravener may feel a moment's relief before realizing they're standing above a network of already-completed passages full of incoming Hormagaunts.

Pyrovore

The Pyrovore is a living chemical weapon — a stocky, heavily-built organism whose internal biology is dedicated almost entirely to the production and storage of violently reactive bio-compounds. Its stomach cavities contain separate reservoirs of precursor fluids that mix in the glandular sacs behind its maw, producing a burning plasma discharge that it vomits across enemies in a searing cone. The creature radiates heat even at rest, its skin warm to the touch and faintly glowing at the joints where internal pressure builds.

What makes the Pyrovore truly dangerous is not just the Flamespurt — it is the creature itself. Its internal chemical reservoirs destabilize catastrophically upon death, and the resulting explosion has ended more than one overconfident assault. Warriors who close to melee with a Pyrovore solve one problem while creating another: the creature is designed to die loudly. Tyranid commanders deploy Pyrovores as mobile incendiary platforms against fortified positions, tunnel networks, and any environment where the psychological effect of fire compounds the physical damage. The Hive Mind does not fear losing them. It expects to.

Lictor

The Lictor is not sent to fight a battle — it is sent to ensure the enemy never knows the battle is coming. These chameleonic infiltrators slip through enemy lines weeks or months before the main swarm, scouting positions, marking targets, assassinating isolated sentries, and seeding the air with pheromones that guide the swarm's advance. When they finally reveal themselves in combat, they are devastatingly effective killers.

The Lictor's chameleonic skin cells respond to light across the full visible spectrum and into infrared, rendering it effectively invisible while stationary and deeply difficult to track while moving. Its feeder tendrils — hollow sensory organs that press into the skulls of its victims — extract encoded biological data and transmit it directly into the Hive Mind. Tactical formations, weapon emplacements, the locations of commanders, evacuation routes: all consumed and shared in the moments after a kill. By the time the main swarm arrives, the Hive Mind knows every defensive position as intimately as the defenders themselves. Lictors are the reason Tyranid assaults seem uncanny in their precision — the intelligence was purchased in silence, weeks before the first Hormagaunt was seen.

Tyranid Warrior

Tyranid Warriors are the tactical backbone of any Tyranid ground offensive — smarter, tougher, and more adaptable than the swarm-gaunts they lead. Each Warrior is a living weapons platform, bioengineered with multiple attack forms and a Synapse field that keeps lesser creatures coherent and disciplined around them. They are the Hive Mind's sergeants: capable independent fighters who also make everything around them more dangerous.
