CHAOS UNDIVIDED BESTIARY

Warhammer 40,000 × Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition — Homebrew

Not all who serve Chaos bow to a single god. Some warlords are too proud, too ambitious, or simply too useful to be claimed by any one Ruinous Power — and so they are claimed by all four, or by none. These are the Chaos Undivided: Heretic Astartes warbands and their mortal followers who carry the eight-pointed star, who fight in Chaos's name without submitting to a patron's specific aesthetic. They are no less dangerous for their eclecticism. A Chaos Lord who answers to all four gods is one who can make deals with all four, draw from all four, and be constrained by none. Entries are arranged from lowest to highest Challenge Rating, from the desperate Chaos Cultist to the transcendent Daemon Prince.

These are fan-made rules for personal use only.

Designer's Notes: Key Chaos Undivided Mechanics

Chaos Power Armor. The corrupted plate worn by Heretic Astartes is both tougher and stranger than its Loyalist equivalent. It grants resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical weapons, and critical hits from such weapons become normal hits. However, the Warp energy suffusing it renders the wearer visible to psychic senses — a Chaos Space Marine's armor actually makes them easier to detect via psyker abilities, not harder.

Warp Mutation. Chaos creatures who have been sufficiently exposed to the Immaterium develop random mutations that can trigger at key moments. When a Chaos creature with Warp Mutation is reduced to half its maximum hit points for the first time in combat, roll a d6 on the Mutation table: 1 — Bone Spurs (all melee attacks deal +3 piercing damage); 2 — Iron Hide (gains resistance to one damage type of the GM's choice); 3 — Extra Eyes (advantage on Perception checks and can't be surprised); 4 — Warp Shriek (each creature within 20 ft. makes a DC 12 Wisdom save or is frightened until end of its next turn); 5 — Regenerating Flesh (regains 5 HP at the start of each turn); 6 — Unnatural Speed (speed increases by 15 ft. and gains one additional melee attack per turn).

Bolt Weapons. Bolters, bolt pistols, and their variants fire self-propelled mass-reactive rounds that detonate inside the target. On a hit, the target must succeed on a Constitution saving throw (DC listed per weapon) or take additional piercing damage at the start of its next turn as the round detonates. Creatures in sealed void suits or heavily constructed armor (AC 18+) have advantage on this save.

Daemon Prince Ascension. At the GM's discretion, a Chaos Lord who deals the killing blow on a creature of CR 10 or higher may be offered Daemonhood by the Chaos Gods. If the Chaos Lord accepts and succeeds on a DC 18 Charisma saving throw, it immediately transforms into a Daemon Prince and uses that stat block for the remainder of the encounter. If it fails the saving throw, it instead becomes a Chaos Spawn.

Chaos Cultist

Chaos Cultists are the wretched vanguard of any Chaos incursion — ordinary mortals who have surrendered their sanity, loyalty, and often their humanity in exchange for the hollow promise of power, transcendence, or simply survival. They come from every walk of life: factory workers, planetary militia deserters, nobles driven mad by forbidden texts, and entire populations subverted by generations of hidden worship. By the time they take up arms for the Ruinous Powers, they have burned every bridge behind them.

In battle, Cultists are expendable. Their commanders send them in waves to exhaust ammunition, trigger killzones, and draw the attention of elite defenders while Chaos Space Marines maneuver into position. Individual Cultists are poorly equipped and poorly trained, but they feel no fear, they do not rout, and there are always more of them than anyone expects. A planet that has been subverted for long enough can field millions.

Traitor Guard

Not every soldier in the armies of Chaos is a screaming fanatic with improvised weapons. The Traitor Guard — variously called Renegade Militia, Apostate Regiments, or simply Turncoats — are defected Imperial Guardsmen who have brought their training, equipment, and sometimes their entire regimental command structure with them when they crossed over to Chaos. They still fire in disciplined volleys. They still dig defensive positions. They still obey orders. The difference is whose orders they obey, and what those orders ask of them.

Traitor Guard are considerably more dangerous than Chaos Cultists for exactly this reason. A cultist is a fanatical civilian with a knife. A Traitor Guard veteran is a soldier with years of combat experience, standard-issue flak armor, a functioning lasgun, and the ingrained habit of covering their squad's advance. Chaos warlords who inherit intact Traitor Guard regiments treat them as valuable assets — not quite as valuable as Chaos Space Marines, but as close as mortal soldiers can get.

Dark Apostle

A Dark Apostle is a Chaos Space Marine who has abandoned the battlefield disciplines of the Legiones Astartes in favor of something the Emperor spent ten thousand years trying to eradicate: faith. They are the priests of the Long War, the orators whose sermons can send a mob of frightened Cultists charging into autocannon fire with fervor rather than compulsion, the figures who maintain the spiritual infrastructure of a Chaos warband between engagements. Their armor is plastered with heretical scripture and chains of skulls. They carry corrupted crozius staves that crackle with Warp energy. They are never, under any circumstances, quiet.

In combat a Dark Apostle fights at the center of the warband, close enough that every nearby warrior can hear the litanies and feel the Warp energy rolling off the Apostle's conviction. They are not the most dangerous individual combatants a Chaos force fields — that honor goes to the Chaos Lord and the Daemon Prince — but they may be the most dangerous force multiplier. An inspired mob of Chaos Cultists fighting under a Dark Apostle's blessing is considerably more lethal than a rabble would suggest.

Chaos Rhino

The Chaos Rhino is the workhorse of the Traitor Legions — a rugged, boxy armored personnel carrier that has been hauling Heretic Astartes into battle for ten thousand years and shows no signs of stopping. What was once a reliable Imperial transport has, through millennia of Warp exposure and deliberate corruption, become something half-machine and half-vessel-of-Chaos. The hull is tattooed with eight-pointed stars and devotional scripture. The exhaust ports breathe something that smells faintly wrong. The engine note, at idle, sounds almost like a voice.

In battle the Rhino does what it has always done: close the gap. It drives into enemy lines under fire, drops its assault ramp, and disgorges a squad of Chaos Space Marines directly into melee range before any defender has time to reposition. Its twin-linked bolter emplacement lays down suppressive fire on the way in. Its dozer blade clears everything in front of it. It is not a sophisticated weapon — but then, few problems in the Long War require sophistication.

Warpsmith

A Warpsmith is what happens when a Chaos Space Marine with the Techmarine's gift for machine-bonding has spent centuries in the Warp, and both he and the machines he tends have absorbed the corruption of the Immaterium until neither is entirely recognizable as what they were. Where a Loyalist Techmarine maintains and repairs, a Warpsmith corrupts and perverts. They walk the battlefield wreathed in mechadendrites that crackle with eldritch energy, capable of repairing their own machines mid-engagement, disabling enemy vehicles with a touch, and calling down the obliterating fire of their servo-automata on whatever the warband needs destroyed next.

Warpsmiths are deeply practical individuals in a faction that tends toward excess and transcendence — they believe that a properly maintained and properly corrupted war machine is more reliable than a daemon patron's blessing, and history has largely vindicated them on this point. They hate Loyalist Techmarines with a professional contempt that is entirely separate from the ordinary hatred most Heretic Astartes feel, and they reserve special attention for enemy vehicles, which they view as machines that have been allowed to remain clean.

Chaos Raptors

Chaos Raptors are jump-pack assault troops who have fully embraced the predator fantasy their equipment enables. Where Loyalist Assault Marines descend in controlled formation, Raptors fall on their prey screaming, spinning, firing pistols in every direction and crashing into melee with a force that has broken heavier lines than theirs. They have cultivated their own cultural identity within the Traitor Legions, a mythology of the hunt that some have taken so literally they file their teeth and compete to see who can bring down the most impressive quarry in a single engagement.

Jump pack technology in Chaos hands is, like everything else, both more and less reliable than the Loyalist version. The packs burn hotter, accelerate faster, and have a documented tendency to explode if maintained according to standard protocols rather than the idiosyncratic rituals each Raptor develops. The Raptors themselves consider this proof of the packs' character. Everything worth flying with, they say, tries to kill you.

Chaos Aspiring Champion

An Aspiring Champion is the squad leader of a Chaos Space Marine unit — a position earned not through appointment but through the simple and final method of being the last one left alive with a credible claim to it. They have killed their predecessors, inspired or intimidated their squadmates into obedience, and drawn enough of the Chaos Gods' attention that their weapons are beginning to develop personalities and their armor has started to whisper. They are not yet Chosen, not yet old enough to be called veterans in any meaningful sense of the word, but they are ambitious in the particular Chaos fashion: they would like to become Chaos Lords, they understand that this requires surviving, and they are prepared to do absolutely anything necessary to accomplish both goals.

In combat, an Aspiring Champion pushes to the front. Status in a Chaos warband is measured in kills, and an Aspiring Champion who lets his squad do his killing for him will not remain an Aspiring Champion for long. They fight with a focused savagery that differentiates them from the rank-and-file — not reckless, but hungry in a way that makes them push harder and recover faster than ordinary Chaos Space Marines.

Chaos Space Marine

A Chaos Space Marine is what ten millennia of war, Warp exposure, and hatred produce when they are given a genetically engineered superhuman body and pointed at everything they once swore to protect. The Heretic Astartes who fought in the Horus Heresy are ancient by mortal standards — the oldest veterans remember the Emperor's face, the sound of his voice, what it felt like to believe. That memory curdled long ago into something that drives them harder than any ideology: pure, focused, undying resentment.

In battle they fight with the same discipline and lethality as their Loyalist counterparts, with two additions: they do not restrain themselves, and they do not stop. Bolters fire until the barrel glows. Chainblades swing until the teeth are stripped. A Chaos Space Marine does not break, does not surrender, and does not feel the fear that would prompt either. The only variable is which god — if any — they have pledged themselves to, which determines exactly what kind of nightmare they have become.

Chaos Spawn

A Chaos Spawn is what happens when the gifts of the Ruinous Powers become too numerous to contain in a single body — when a Chaos champion's mutations accumulate past the point of any coherent form. What was once a human or Astartes becomes a writhing mass of mismatched limbs, sensory organs, maws, and lashing appendages, the mind within it long since dissolved into inarticulate hunger. The Spawn retains just enough cognition to pursue and attack, and nothing else.

Champions of Chaos know what the Spawn represents: a cautionary example and, sometimes, a weapon. Some warlords collect pens of them and release them ahead of their warbands as shock troops or living obstacles. The Spawn cannot be given orders. It can only be pointed. There is something darkly appropriate about a being consumed by the Warp becoming, in the end, a tool of those who have not yet been similarly consumed.

Havocs

Havocs are Chaos Space Marines who have specialized entirely in heavy weapons — soldiers who have traded the mobility and versatility of the standard tactical role for the ability to destroy vehicles, fortifications, and concentrated infantry formations from positions that most armies would consider outside effective range. They are the Traitor Legion's answer to the problem of things that won't die when you shoot them normally. When a warband encounters enemy armor, walls, or anything that requires firepower measured in kilowatts, the Havocs are called forward.

Each Havoc chooses a weapon that matches their temperament and their idea of what destruction looks like. Lascannon operators tend toward patience — they wait, they track, they make each shot count. Heavy bolter crews are the opposite, pinning entire squads in place with sustained fire that nobody sane would walk through. Missile launcher Havocs are pragmatists who appreciate versatility. Autocannon Havocs are, in the assessment of their fellow Heretic Astartes, probably enjoying this too much. All of them are extremely effective.

Chaos Lord in Terminator Armor

Some Chaos Lords have survived long enough to recover or plunder a suit of Terminator armor, and have worn it long enough that the suit has bonded to them in the distinctly Chaos fashion — not the dormant machine spirit of a Loyalist suit, but something awake and invested in the survival of the being it encases. A Chaos Lord in Terminator Armor is among the most individually durable entities a ground force can encounter: the armor soaks damage that would kill an unprotected Chaos Lord twice over, and the Lord within has the experience to use that durability tactically rather than simply standing in place and absorbing punishment.

They teleport. This is the fact that defines every tactical engagement that includes one. A Chaos Lord in Terminator Armor who is engaged at one point on the line can be at a completely different point on the line four seconds later, and the defenders at the new point are generally not ready for this. The armor's teleportation system was designed for strategic insertion behind enemy lines; that it can also be used to ambush command elements, assassinate support weapons, and appear behind prepared defensive positions is something that every Chaos Lord in Terminator Armor has thoroughly explored.

Daemon Prince

A Daemon Prince is the ultimate reward offered by the Chaos Gods to their mortal champions: the final transaction in a lifetime of service, murder, and sacrifice. The champion's mortal body is torn apart and rebuilt as something divine — a being of living Warp energy encased in a physical form of terrifying power. They retain their memories, their personality, and their tactical capabilities, combined now with the supernatural resilience and strength of a true daemon. They do not age. They do not tire. They are not afraid of anything in the material realm, because they have seen what lives beyond it.

A Daemon Prince Undivided serves all four gods — or none of them, which amounts to the same thing from the perspective of a mortal on the wrong end of its daemon blade. They carry the favor of every Ruinous Power simultaneously, drawing on each as the moment demands. This makes them versatile in a way their god-specific counterparts are not: capable of sorcery, of brutal melee dominance, of command and of terror, often in the same engagement. They are the apex of what Chaos aspires to produce from mortal flesh.
